## The role of AI in design systems

AI is changing how we design and build products. It helps teams move faster across design and development — from generating first drafts to scaffolding entire interfaces from a prompt.

But AI is only as good as the context you give it.

You can use AI without a design system. But without a clear source of truth, outputs drift. Wrong components, mismatched patterns, decisions that don't align across products. What looks right in isolation breaks down at scale — and the fix always costs more than the speed gain was worth.

A design system changes that dynamic. It defines the components, patterns, and behaviours that guide both humans and AI. Instead of checking whether outputs are correct at the end, the rules shape what gets generated in the first place. The design system stops being a reference and becomes a foundation — one that AI actively builds from.

You can build your own, but it takes real ongoing effort. Defining standards, maintaining consistency, and meeting accessibility requirements aren't one-time tasks. SGDS gives you a ready foundation that reflects government standards and established patterns aligned to the Digital Service Standards, so outputs are usable, accessible, and consistent across government products. We've also introduced AI support to keep you aligned with SGDS as you build — so the speed AI gives you doesn't come at the cost of quality or coherence.

#### What this means for SGDS moving forward

As AI becomes part of everyday workflows, design systems need to work not just for people, but for AI as well. This shifts how systems need to be designed — they have to be clear, structured, and interpretable, so they can guide both human decisions and AI-generated outputs equally well.

The way product teams work is already changing. Workflows that were once linear — design, then hand off, then build — are becoming fluid. Today, you can start anywhere and go everywhere.

SGDS is evolving with this shift. We're continuing to build a system that works for the way teams work now — so that when you reach for AI, what gets built back is consistent, considered, and worth shipping.
